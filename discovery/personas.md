# Personas

Two personas, behavioural rather than demographic. Age, income and job title are left out
deliberately — none of them predict what a person does when they cannot find a bay.

The programme requires that this be useful to a named person. It is: the primary persona
is a real person, named, who hit this problem on 2026-09-21 and whose response to it is
the reason the project exists.

---

## Primary — "parks defensively"

**Named person: Amrutha Korumilli** (Hyderabad; this project's author, and the first user).

**The behaviour that defines her:** she decides where to put the car *before* she leaves,
not on arrival. When there is no plan, she does not circle — she changes the trip, or she
finds a private lot she has some social claim on. On 2026-09-21 that meant leaving the car
at her gym, because the gym had space and she is a member. She optimises for the car being
*safe and not in anyone's way*, not for the shortest walk.

**What she does today**
- Thinks about parking as part of deciding whether to make the trip at all
- Will accept a longer walk in exchange for certainty
- Treats blocking a lane as not an option, even when others do it
- Relies on informal access — a gym, a friend's building, a shop she knows

**What she needs from this product**
- To know, before leaving, that a specific bay is hers for a specific window
- A price she can see up front, per hour, no surprises on exit
- Confidence that the building will actually let her in when she arrives

**What would make her abandon it**
- Turning up with a booking and being refused at the gate. One occurrence kills it.
- Needing to phone anyone
- Discovering the price at exit rather than at booking

**Why she is primary:** she is the acute case. Her problem is nightly and structural (no
building parking, a lane too narrow to use), not occasional. If the product does not work
for her it does not work.

## Secondary — "decides on arrival"

**Behaviour:** drives to a crowded destination without a parking plan, and solves it in the
last two minutes. Circles, takes a chance on the street, or hands the keys to whoever is
standing nearest. Tolerates a surprising amount of friction because it has always been
like this and there has never been an alternative.

**What they do today**
- Arrive, then look
- Will double-park or block briefly if the errand is short
- Pay cash to an attendant without asking the rate
- Give up and drive home if it takes more than a few minutes

**What they need from this product**
- To be findable *at the moment of arrival*, not just in advance — a fast "what is free
  near me right now" answer
- Booking that takes seconds, not a signup flow
- To not feel like the slow option compared with chancing it

**What would make them abandon it**
- Any signup wall before seeing whether a spot even exists
- Being slower than circling
- Empty results — two failed looks and they stop opening it

**Why they are secondary, not primary:** this persona is the larger group but the harder
sell, because their current behaviour is free and immediate. Serving them well needs
density of supply the first release will not have. The product is aimed at the primary
persona first and should not pretend otherwise; see `plan/scope.md`.

---

## Who is deliberately not a persona

**The commuter.** An early framing of this project targeted the IT corridor as a
workplace-parking problem. It was wrong — people driving to their own office are given
parking by that office. Recorded here because it was a real wrong turn, and the correction
changed the seed data and the pitch.

**The building manager / supply side.** A real and necessary persona for the full product,
and the one carrying the hardest problem (trust, liability, gate access). Out of scope for
the first release, which seeds supply by hand. Named here so the omission is a decision
rather than an oversight.
